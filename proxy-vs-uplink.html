Getting the Mac online through the phone

Two ways to borrow the phone's internet

Same goal either way — your Mac gets online through the phone over the USB cable. The real difference is who has to know about it: every app, or nobody.

Now · proxy mode

You tell each app, one by one

Like walking desk to desk asking each person to write the phone's address on their mail.

The catch: you have to configure every app, stubborn ones (Chrome) ignore you, and a new window forgets the setting. Miss one and it leaks onto normal Wi-Fi without telling you.

Option 2 · uplink mode

One checkpoint catches everything

Leave everyone alone. Put one clerk at the exit who re-routes every letter through the phone.

Your apps — untouched, all just normal

No app is configured. They don't even know the road changed. Every window, every new terminal, Chrome included — all covered automatically.

pfmacOS's built-in traffic controller — the doorway that catches every request on its way out.
redsocksa small helper you install — the clerk that actually hands each request to the phone.

The whole idea in one line: proxy mode configures each app; uplink mode changes the shared road. Apps can't be stubborn about a road they don't know they're on — so the only thing that changes is where the internet comes from.
